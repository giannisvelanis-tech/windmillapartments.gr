Clean Monday in Galaxidi

Alevromoutzouroma: the Galaxidi flour war

On Clean Monday the rest of Greece flies kites. Galaxidi fills with coloured flour. Locals and visitors head down to the harbour and pelt one another with it, to music and dancing, until the whole village is painted.

In 2027: Monday 15 March. Clean Monday moves every year, because it depends on Orthodox Easter.

Clean Monday 2024 in Galaxidi.

What happens

The flour battle starts in the morning at the harbour. The flour fighters, many in costume, throw flour dyed every colour and smear each other's faces. The main "battle" lasts a few hours; the party, with music, food and dancing, often goes on into the night. About 1,700 people live in Galaxidi, but thousands of visitors come for the day.

A little history

People in Galaxidi say their ancestors brought the custom home on their sailing ships around 1800, and that it exists nowhere else. The year 1801 is often given. Others link it to similar customs in Sicily, seen by Galaxidi sailors in the 19th century, or to Byzantium. Nobody knows for sure.

Under Ottoman rule, people waited for Carnival to celebrate in masks or with faces blackened with charcoal. Flour, laundry blue, ochre and shoe polish came later. At first only men took part; today everyone does.

Tips

  • Wear clothes and shoes you don't mind ruining. Dyed flour doesn't always wash out.
  • Keep your phone and camera in a plastic bag.
  • Old custom says that anyone with a smudge or a cross of shoe polish on their forehead is spared. Don't count on it: if you're at the harbour, you'll get floured.
  • Come on the Friday or Saturday. Accommodation in Galaxidi fills up early for the Carnival long weekend.
